The Art of the Perfect Light

Lighting a cigar looks simple until you watch someone do it badly — jamming the flame straight into the foot, puffing furiously, ending up with a lopsided burn that smokes hot on one side for the rest of the session. A good light takes an extra thirty seconds. It's worth it every time.

Think toasting, not torching

The best way to picture the process is roasting a marshmallow over a campfire: hold the cigar just above the flame, close enough that it dances up toward the tobacco, but never letting fire actually touch it. Direct contact scorches the wrapper and makes the cigar burn too hot too fast.

Rotate as you go

Slowly turn the cigar while it's above the flame so the entire foot heats evenly. You're looking for a complete, glowing ring around the tip with lightly blackened edges — not just one charred spot. This patience up front is what prevents an uneven "canoeing" burn later in the smoke, where one side burns faster than the other.

Take the first draw once it's fully lit

With the foot evenly toasted, bring the cigar to your mouth and draw gently while watching the ember. It should glow evenly across the whole tip. If one side lags, don't worry — just touch it up with a quick pass of the flame before you settle in.

Choose your flame source wisely

A butane torch lighter is the standard for a reason: it burns clean, with no fuel taste to interfere with the tobacco. If you're going the traditional route with matches, use long, sulfur-free wooden matches (paper matches and short kitchen matches won't do), and let the sulfur burn off completely before bringing the flame near the cigar. Avoid candles, oil-based lighters, and standard Zippos if you can — they can impart flavors you didn't order.

Get the light right and everything downstream is easier: a more even burn, better flavor from the first third to the last, and no fighting a canoe with your cutter or lighter twenty minutes in. It's the one step in cigar smoking where a little patience pays off for the entire session.
