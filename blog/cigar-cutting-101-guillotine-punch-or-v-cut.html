Cigar Cutting 101: Guillotine, Punch, or V-Cut?

Every cigar comes capped — a small piece of wrapper leaf sealed over the head to keep the filler from unraveling before you buy it. Before you can light up, that cap has to come off, and how you remove it actually changes how the cigar smokes. Here's what the three main tools do differently.

Guillotine cutters are the standard for a reason

A single clean stroke slices straight across the head, opening a wide, even draw. The trick is speed and confidence: a slow, hesitant cut is more likely to tear the wrapper than a quick, decisive one. Double-bladed guillotines have a slight edge over single-blade versions here, since both sides of the cut close simultaneously, which reduces the risk of pinching or crushing the head. General rule of thumb: cut about a sixteenth of an inch above the cap, just enough to open things up without slicing into the binder underneath.

Punch cutters take a different approach entirely

Instead of removing the cap, they punch a small circular hole into it. This gives you a tighter, more concentrated draw with less loose tobacco coming loose in your mouth, and it's nearly impossible to mess up. The tradeoff is that a punch narrows your airflow more than a full guillotine cut, and it won't work at all on cigars with pointed, torpedo-style heads.

V-cutters split the difference

Instead of a straight line, they carve a wedge-shaped notch into the cap, which opens a fuller draw than a punch while staying more controlled than a full guillotine slice. Some smokers feel a V-cut concentrates flavor onto the tongue in an interesting way. It does take a slightly steadier hand than a guillotine to get right.

So which one should you use?

Honestly, there's no wrong answer — it comes down to preference and the cigar in front of you. Guillotines are the safe, versatile default. Punches are foolproof for parejo (straight-sided) cigars when you want a tight draw. V-cutters are worth trying once you've got the basics down and want to experiment.

Whatever you use, keep the blade sharp — a dull cutter tears the wrapper instead of slicing it, and a torn cap is the fastest way to ruin a good cigar before you've even lit it.
